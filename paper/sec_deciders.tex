\subsection{The same behaviour across six models}\label{sec:deciders}

Is this Clef's behaviour, or how System One models behave as deciders? Table~\ref{tab:exam} gives every model's
answers to the fixed exam. On order steps all six pick a right option far more often than the LLM's own sample (Clef
\EXaccClef\%, pplx-decider-v1.2 \EXaccPplx\%, d3 \EXaccDthree\%, linne-oak-1.0 \EXaccLinne\%, decider-31b
\EXaccMapika\%, amos-1-large \EXaccAmos\%; the LLM \EXaccFirst\%, random \EXaccRandom\%), and all of them also order
when no required order is on offer (\EXnoneAmos--\EXnonePplx\% of such steps, the LLM \EXnoneFirst\%). They agree with
each other more than with the LLM (pplx-decider-v1.2 and d3 on \EXpairPplxDthree\% of questions, Clef and
pplx-decider-v1.2 on \EXpairClefPplx\%). Online, pplx-decider-v1.2 in \sel{} keeps the LLM's checkpoint level
(\ONSelPplxcp{} points over \ONSelPplxn{} tasks) and in \tool{} falls far behind (\ONToolPplxcp{} points over
\ONToolPplxn{} tasks; Table~\ref{tab:deciders}). The commitment bias, its benefit on orders and its failure when
steering are shared by the models we could test, not a quirk of Clef.
